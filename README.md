This repository contains an end-to-end demonstration of Microsoft Fabric capabilities for cross-domain hospital intelligence, integrating Materialized Lake Views, Fabric IQ Ontology, Data Agents, and Activator against simulated electronic health record, staffing, census, and billing data. The demo is organized around a three-workspace architecture in which source data, operational intelligence, and decision-and-action layers are separated by governance boundaries while remaining connected through cross-workspace Materialized Lake Views. Synthetic data spanning a multi-hospital network is generated by reproducible notebooks, and all transformations are expressed declaratively in Spark SQL so that dependency ordering and refresh behavior remain inspectable. Agent instructions, ontology definitions, and Activator configuration are provided alongside the data and transformation assets to support reproducible deployment and evaluation.

## Dataset Structure

The dataset is organized as a layered medallion model spanning three governance-separated workspaces, with each layer materialized as Delta tables by Materialized Lake Views. The structure reflects the project's partitioning by governance domain and refresh cadence rather than by analytic convenience. Raw source tables reside in the source-of-truth workspace, operational intelligence MLVs reside in the clinical operations workspace, and decision-and-action MLVs reside in the command center workspace. Cross-workspace reads are expressed directly in Spark SQL; no staging copies or hand-built ETL artifacts are introduced between layers.

### Source Layer

The source layer contains six tables that simulate hospital systems of record. Each table is keyed to a defined grain, and referential relationships to dimension tables (hospitals, wards, regions, DRGs, payers) are maintained across the set.

| Table | Grain | Simulated system |
|---|---|---|
| `raw_admissions` | Patient encounter | EHR admission feed (diagnosis, DRG, acuity, comorbidity index) |
| `raw_discharges` | Patient encounter | EHR discharge feed (disposition, LOS, follow-up status, medication reconciliation) |
| `raw_readmissions` | Readmission event | 30-day readmission tracking with days-since-discharge |
| `raw_ward_census` | Ward × hour | Real-time bed census feed |
| `raw_staffing` | Ward × shift | Staffing system (nurse count, nurse-patient ratio per shift) |
| `raw_billing` | Claim / encounter | Revenue cycle (DRG payment, total cost, payer program) |

Dimension tables — hospitals, wards, regions, DRG definitions, and payer programs — are generated alongside the fact tables and serve as the join backbone across all downstream MLVs.

### Operational Intelligence Layer

Operational MLVs read cross-workspace from the source layer and compute ward- and encounter-level operational state. The layer contains five MLVs:

- `ward_utilization` — daily occupancy percentage, peak hours, and available beds per ward.
- `staffing_adequacy` — nurse-patient ratio against ward-type safe thresholds (1:4 Med-Surg, 1:2 ICU), by ward × shift.
- `los_patterns` — average length of stay by DRG versus expected, with outlier flags.
- `readmission_flags` — 30-day readmission identification linked to the original encounter, ward, and DRG.
- `discharge_risk_factors` — per-discharge risk profile combining staffing at discharge, census pressure, follow-up status, and comorbidity.

This layer unifies three distinct grains — encounter, ward-shift, and claim — declaratively in SQL, producing a consistent key space for downstream consumption.

### Decision & Action Layer

Decision MLVs read cross-workspace from the operational layer and are partitioned into two functional groups.

Pattern detection MLVs — addressing what happened and why:

- `hospital_quality_scorecard` — readmission rate, LOS variance, and patient safety composite by hospital.
- `staffing_outcome_correlation` — wards where understaffing correlates with above-average readmissions.
- `readmission_financial_impact` — estimated excess readmission cost and DRG margin erosion.
- `cost_per_patient` — cost breakdown by DRG, hospital, and payer, with margin analysis.

Risk-signal MLVs — addressing what requires attention now:

- `unsafe_discharge_risk` — patients discharged today from understaffed wards with high comorbidity and no scheduled follow-up.
- `capacity_pressure` — wards above 85% occupancy or below-threshold staffing on the current shift.

### Consumption Layer

Three Direct Lake semantic models — one per agent persona — read the decision-layer Delta tables directly, without an import pipeline. A Fabric IQ Ontology registers the business entities and relationships traversed by the agents (Discharge Event → Ward → Staffing Adequacy → Patient Encounter → DRG → Discharge Risk Factors → Hospital → Readmission Financial Impact), enabling cross-domain queries that span the clinical, operational, and financial layers. Column-level and row-level security rules are applied at the semantic-model layer and propagate to agent responses.

### Generation Parameters

All data is synthetic and generated by deterministic notebooks. The simulated network comprises 12 hospitals across 4 U.S. regions, 5 wards per hospital (ICU, Med-Surg, Surgical, Pediatrics, Emergency), approximately 710 beds, approximately 50,000 admissions over an 18-month window, approximately 788,000 ward-hour census rows, and approximately 98,000 staffing shift rows. No real patient data, protected health information, or proprietary operational data is included.

### Derived Materialized Lake Views

MLVs in `ws-zava-clinical-ops` inherit and align grains from the source tables. Key derived fields include:

- **`ward_utilization`** — `ward_id`, `census_date`, `occupancy_pct`, `peak_hour`, `available_beds`.
- **`staffing_adequacy`** — `ward_id`, `shift_start_ts`, `nurse_patient_ratio`, `safe_threshold`, `is_understaffed` (boolean flag computed against ward-type thresholds: 1:4 Med-Surg, 1:2 ICU).
- **`los_patterns`** — `encounter_id`, `drg_code`, `expected_los`, `actual_los`, `los_variance`, `is_outlier`.
- **`readmission_flags`** — `original_encounter_id`, `readmission_id`, `days_since_discharge`, `ward_id`, `drg_code`.
- **`discharge_risk_factors`** — `encounter_id`, `discharge_ts`, `ward_id`, `is_understaffed_at_discharge`, `census_pressure`, `follow_up_scheduled`, `comorbidity_index`, `composite_risk_score`.

MLVs in `ws-zava-command-center` aggregate these into persona-facing schemas. `hospital_quality_scorecard`, `staffing_outcome_correlation`, `readmission_financial_impact`, and `cost_per_patient` expose hospital-level, ward-level, and DRG-level rollups. `unsafe_discharge_risk` preserves encounter grain to support patient-level risk review, and `capacity_pressure` preserves ward × shift grain to support real-time operational alerts.

Columns not enumerated here are either passthrough identifiers from upstream tables or computed convenience fields documented inline in the corresponding `.sql` notebook (`10-clinical-ops-mlvs.sql`, `20-command-center-mlvs.sql`, `21-risk-signal-mlvs.sql`). All timestamps are UTC. All monetary amounts are stored as decimal in U.S. dollars without currency conversion. No column contains real patient data; all identifiers and clinical values are synthetically generated.

## Source

The dataset is entirely synthetic and was generated programmatically for demonstration and reproducibility purposes. No real patient records, hospital operations data, staffing logs, or financial records were used at any stage of construction. Every identifier, diagnosis, discharge event, staffing ratio, census reading, and claim amount originates from a deterministic generator whose parameters are fixed and auditable.

Generation proceeds in four ordered stages, each corresponding to a source domain simulated in the demo. First, dimension tables for hospitals, wards, regions, and diagnosis-related groups are established as a stable reference frame. Second, admission, discharge, and readmission events are synthesized at the patient-encounter grain, with diagnosis assignment, acuity, and comorbidity indices drawn from bounded distributions calibrated to the intended clinical scenarios. Third, hourly ward census and per-shift staffing records are produced at the ward-hour and ward-shift grains respectively, with occupancy and nurse-to-patient ratios constrained to plausible operational ranges. Fourth, billing and claims records are generated at the encounter grain and linked to the corresponding admissions, with payment and cost figures derived from the assigned diagnosis-related group.

All four stages draw from a single fixed random seed to guarantee reproducibility: executing the generation notebooks in order yields byte-identical tables. Because the source tables are synthetic, the demo intentionally does not model real-world data quality defects beyond those explicitly introduced for illustration, and the generators do not attempt to reproduce the full complexity of production EHR, staffing, census, or revenue-cycle systems. Downstream Materialized Lake Views, semantic models, and agent configurations in this repository operate exclusively on these generated tables; no external data connection or ingestion path is required or implied.
